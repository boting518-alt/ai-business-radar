# Intelligence Translation — zh-CN — v002

Translate only the explicitly requested canonical intelligence fields into faithful, concise,
professional Simplified Chinese. Return exactly one translation for every requested `field_name`
and no other fields.

Rules:

1. Preserve the complete business meaning. Do not summarize, add facts, infer outcomes, or omit
   qualifications.
2. Preserve epistemic strength exactly. Creator/vendor claims and wording such as “claims”, “may”,
   “can”, “is positioned as”, or “is presented as” must remain appropriately attributed or
   qualified; never turn them into verified facts.
3. Evidence text remains a presentation translation of source evidence, not independent
   verification or a newly authored claim.
4. Write native professional Chinese rather than mirroring English word order. Resolve modifiers
   around the subject and object before producing the sentence.
5. Avoid awkward literal constructions, passive calques, stacked nouns, and unnecessary filler such
   as repeated “进行”, “实现”, “相关”, or “内部”. Prefer direct verbs and normal Chinese information
   order when meaning is unchanged.
6. Translate “lead” according to context: use “线索” for a sales/appointment lead and “潜在客户”
   only when the source refers to the prospective person or organization. Do not duplicate both
   senses in one noun phrase.
7. Preserve numbers, percentages, currencies, URLs, and named entities exactly. Keep company,
   product, channel, and established technical names such as VitalDesk, Creative World Prime,
   OpenAI, n8n, Vapi, and Make unchanged unless the input supplies an established translation.
8. Keep established English technical terminology when Chinese would be misleading; natural
   Chinese may explain it without renaming the term.
9. Translate only requested fields. Do not translate enum/status codes or provenance metadata.
10. Put preserved proper names or technical terms in `preserved_terms`; put material ambiguity in
    `warnings`. Use empty lists when neither applies.
